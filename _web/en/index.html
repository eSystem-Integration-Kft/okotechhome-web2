Looking for a solution for an institution, a plant or a larger community?

At higher loads we do not pick a single domestic unit; we design a properly sized treatment system — whether for an institution, a plant, an accommodation business or a community of several properties, such as a holiday complex, a residential park or a smaller part of a settlement. Technologies

Collecting, settling or treating wastewater
— the three are not the same thing

Several kinds of system can come into play on a property without a sewer, but they do not do the same job.

  • 01

    A sealed holding tank collects; it does not treat. It stores the wastewater until the next emptying; no treatment happens inside it and no treated water is produced — nothing leaves the system except by tanker. There is no biological process to maintain, so it is indifferent to whether the property is used evenly or only occasionally: after a long gap it works exactly as before. In return it needs regular emptying, at a frequency set by the tank's size and the actual water use — and the running cost follows from that. It matters that the tanker can reach the tank safely. Its place is where local conditions permit nothing to enter the ground at all, and no other solution is possible.

  • 02

    A septic tank treats anaerobically; treatment is completed in the soil. Solids settle in the tank while the breakdown of organic matter begins in an oxygen-free environment. Treatment does not finish here, though: a significant part of the process takes place in the drainage field, with the help of the soil's natural microorganisms. It therefore works efficiently only with a drainage field of adequate size, typically two to three times that of a biological treatment unit. It runs by gravity, without electricity; but depending on how intensively it is used it generally needs emptying every 1–3 years, and most manufacturers also prescribe dosing with a bacterial preparation to keep the biological processes going. It can be a good solution above all for properties used intermittently or at lower loads.

  • 03

    A biological treatment unit treats inside the unit itself. Microorganisms living in the system break down the organic matter in the wastewater in the presence of air (oxygen); aeration gives them optimal living conditions, so treatment runs continuously and efficiently. The result is treated water suitable for infiltration and for reed-bed use. Operation needs electricity to run the aeration system — thanks to which the bacterial culture sustains itself naturally, so under normal operation no regular dosing with bacterial preparations is required. It is recommended above all for properties in permanent occupation, where the system can work most efficiently at an even load; an ordinary holiday or an absence of a few weeks is not a problem.

Our wastewater solutions

Epureco

Settling tank (septic tank) · distributed product

This is not a wastewater treatment unit: it retains solids and begins the breakdown of organic matter in an oxygen-free environment, but most of the treatment takes place in the drainage field, with the help of the soil's microorganisms. It therefore works efficiently only together with a drainage field of adequate size. It runs by gravity, without electricity; depending on use it typically needs emptying every 1–3 years, along with the bacterial preparation the manufacturer prescribes.

We recommend it for properties with low or erratic loads — a holiday home or a weekend house, for example. Its advantage there is simpler operation that tolerates fluctuating load better. It is also a compromise: a significant part of the treatment happens in the soil, and so at lower treatment performance than A.B. Clear's active biological treatment.

A.B. Clear

Biological treatment unit · our own product

It treats the wastewater inside the unit: microorganisms living in the aerated system break down organic matter in the presence of oxygen. The process ends in treated water suitable for infiltration and reed-bed use. We recommend it for family homes and for regularly used properties. An absence of a few weeks typically causes no problem, but optimal operation needs use of adequate frequency. The unit runs on electricity; with proper operation no regular dosing of bacteria is needed, and thanks to the patented sludge-bag technology it needs no emptying by tanker.

How A.B. Clear works

A biological treatment unit works on the same principle
as a municipal treatment plant

The bacterial culture living in the tank breaks down the organic matter in the wastewater. At the end of the process treated water leaves the unit, suitable for reed-bed infiltration. The entire treatment process takes place in a single tank installed below ground, with no daily intervention.

A.B. Clear, made in Esztergom, puts this principle to work at both domestic and institutional scale. A.B. Clear systems are available up to a capacity of 1–50 people; we determine the size a given property needs from its usage data. Where more capacity is required, the system can be built from several units.

The treatment process

  1. 1 · The wastewater arrives — All the domestic wastewater the household produces enters the unit: both the blackwater from the toilet and the greywater from laundry, washing-up and washing.
  2. 2 · Biological treatment — The bacterial culture living in the unit breaks down the organic matter in the wastewater. Aerated and settling stages alternate, so alongside the organic load some of the nitrogen compounds are removed too. The culture is self-sustaining: it needs neither additives nor replenishment.
  3. 3 · Handling the sludge — The surplus sludge produced during treatment collects in a bag, dewatered and stabilised. The patented sludge-bag technology is what makes this possible. Emptying the bag is a simple operation, and the dried sludge can be composted. This is why A.B. Clear units need no tanker emptying.
  4. 4 · Discharging the treated water — The treated water returns to the environment through reed-bed infiltration.

Patented sludge-bag technology

Installability

What can affect installability?

On most properties the unit can be installed in the usual way. There are, however, a few conditions that call for a separate technical solution, and some that rule it out. These are worth settling before the decision.

On a property supplied from its own well, water quality testing is also required.

What could affect installability on your plot?

Can be installed in the usual way
Where the plot's conditions call for no departure from the standard arrangement.
Can be installed, but needs an extra technical solution
For example with high groundwater or poorly draining, clay soil. Here the treated water can be disposed of through a properly built raised soakaway. A separate solution is also needed where the terrain and the depth of the outgoing pipe mean a gravity arrangement will not work. These are not exclusions, but they affect the build and the cost.
May be an exclusion
Where there is not enough area for a soakaway of the required size, and no other suitable means of water disposal. How much area is needed can be determined together with us, from the property's conditions and the expected load. It is likewise an exclusion if local regulations do not permit installation. In such cases a sealed holding tank is usually the workable route — and we will say so if that is the situation.

The annual cost of running it

Every technology has an annual cost — the
difference is what it consists of and how it grows

A wastewater system runs for decades. Its real cost is therefore not settled at installation but in the items that recur every year. Those items are made up differently by technology. It also matters whether an item is fixed, or grows along with the size of the household and with service charges.

  • Sealed holding tank (cesspit)

    A single item: emptying. For a household of four or five, at average water use, on the order of HUF 512,000 a year. This item grows in two directions at once: with the size of the household and with rising service charges.

  • Septic tank

    It uses no electricity. Depending on how intensively it is used, emptying is generally due every 1–3 years. The manufacturer also prescribes dosing with a bacterial preparation to keep the biological processes going. The Epureco running costs are set out on the technology page .

  • A.B. Clear — biological treatment unit with a sludge bag

    The compressor's electricity use HUF 24,000, the price of the sludge bag HUF 300 (at most 4 a year are needed), and the membrane replacement pro rata HUF 10,500. For a household of four or five that comes to HUF 35,700a year in total. There is no tanker emptying and no additive: the surplus sludge is collected by the patented sludge bag in compostable form. Apart from the electricity, the items are essentially independent of household size.

Predictable running is not only a matter of the line items

A treatment unit stays reliable in the long run when a specialist is not looking at it for the first time once a fault appears. Our quarterly maintenance plan provides regular professional inspection: settings and the condition of parts can be reviewed before they turn into a breakdown, and emerging deviations corrected on the spot. That is what makes the next ten years plannable too — not only the first.

    Frequently asked questions

    Common questions about standalone wastewater treatment, replacing a cesspit and permitting

    Short answers to the questions that come up most often before a decision. The detailed technical, permitting and operational background is available in the related knowledge base material.

    1Is a permit needed to install a domestic treatment unit?

    The permitting process can differ from one municipality to another. In some places a simple notification is enough; elsewhere a more detailed water-rights or regulatory procedure is required. The first step is always the same: establish the local rules, the disposal of the treated water and the plot's conditions.

    → The permitting process A detailed guide to the steps of the regulatory procedure.

    2What determines whether a treatment unit can be installed on a given plot?

    Above all the disposal of the treated water, the soil's percolation capacity, the groundwater level, the depth of the outgoing pipe and local regulations. Knowing whether there is room for the tank is not enough. You also have to see where the water will go.

    → Plot suitability check What to look at on the plot before deciding.

    3Can a biological treatment unit be installed where groundwater is high?

    High groundwater is not necessarily an exclusion, but it may call for a separate technical arrangement. Here the protection of the tank and the disposal of the treated water have to be designed separately. This is the situation where on-site consultancy can be especially useful, because ground conditions are judged most accurately on site — and it can be requested freely, at any time.

    → High groundwater and concrete-basin installation When a different technical arrangement is needed.

    4Where does the treated water go?

    It typically returns to the environment through infiltration within the plot or reed-bed use. The conditions are set together by the soil, the groundwater, local regulations and the chosen technology.

    → Infiltrating the treated water Where the treated water may go, and on what conditions.

    1What is the difference between a sealed holding tank, a septic tank and a biological treatment unit?

    A sealed holding tank collects the wastewater, so it needs regular emptying. A septic tank settles and partially treats. A biological treatment unit actually performs biological treatment, at the end of which treated water leaves the unit.

    → Comparing the technologies Sealed holding tank, septic tank and biological system side by side.

    2Is a biological treatment unit right for a holiday home or intermittent use?

    Not in every case. The bacterial culture works best under a regular load. At low or erratic loads a septic tank is therefore often the more workable route: a simpler, passive technology that tolerates fluctuating load better. It is important to know that the result is not the same, though: a septic tank mainly settles, and most of the treatment happens in the soil, so at lower treatment performance than active biological treatment.

    → Intermittent use and choosing a technology Holiday and seasonal properties: which solution belongs there.

    3What happens to the old cesspit when switching to a biological unit?

    The existing cesspit has to be dealt with properly: emptying, decommissioning, backfilling or conversion to another use may all arise. This has to be settled before installation, because the old system's location and condition affect the build.

    → Replacing a cesspit When replacement is warranted, and what becomes of the old system.

    1Kell szippantani az A.B. Clear biological treatment unitt?

    No. Thanks to the sludge-bag technology the system needs no regular emptying. The surplus sludge produced during treatment collects in a bag, can be taken out dewatered, and then composted.

    → The sludge-bag technology How it works without a tanker.

    2Does a biological treatment unit smell?

    When it is working properly there is no odour. Biological treatment is an aerated process; it is not based on putrefaction. A strong smell typically points to a fault, an overload or a maintenance problem.

    → Odour and maintenance What a smell indicates, and what to do.

    3Do bacteria, tablets or additives need to be dosed?

    In the A.B. Clear system the bacterial culture is self-sustaining, fed by the incoming wastewater. Under normal use no bacterial tablets, powder or other additive is needed.

    → What may and may not go into the system? The rules of everyday use.

    4What makes up the annual running cost?

    It depends on the technology. With a sealed holding tank the main item is emptying. With biological systems, electricity, parts replacement, maintenance and sludge handling can arise. With A.B. Clear there is no emptying and no additive, so the annual cost is easier to plan.

    → Operation and long-term cost The annual items, by technology.

    1Why can no exact price be given by phone or from a few details?

    The final price does not depend on the unit alone. Capacity counts, as do the earthworks, access to the plot, the depth of the outgoing pipe, the groundwater, the disposal of the treated water, and who carries out the installation. An exact quote can therefore only be given once the property's data is known.

    → Indicative price range calculator An order-of-magnitude range from a few questions.

    2What is worth watching for if I already have a quote?

    Check what the quote covers: the unit only, or installation, earthworks, disposal of the treated water, permitting documentation, commissioning and service back-up as well. Missing items can turn up later as separate costs.

    → Quote checker What the quote covers, and what is missing from it.

    1When is online or on-site consultancy worth requesting?

    Neither is needed for the first discussion: many questions can be settled from the property's data, drawings and photographs. If you would like to go through the site itself with our specialist in more detail, though, online or in-person on-site consultancy can be requested at any time — it is not a condition of a quote, and does not depend on whether we consider it warranted. The online form is quick and saves time, but some conditions — the exact gradient of the ground, for instance — cannot always be judged reliably online. With more complex site conditions, the on-site form therefore gives a more accurate picture.
