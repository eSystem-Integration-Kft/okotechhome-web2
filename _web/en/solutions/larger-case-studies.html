Institutional case studies

Beyond the installation photograph, what matters is how the project got from the data to a working plant — and who has operated it since.

What we look for in a large-project reference

This page shows only institutional, community or larger-capacity systems — it is not mixed with family-home references, because those answer a different question.

Beyond the installation photograph, what matters is how the project got from the data to a working plant: what load it was designed for, what the actual operating profile turned out to be, how the water disposal was solved, what went ahead of the biological stage, and who operates it.

On the industrial claims

What we say only with a reference behind it

Our earlier material mentioned the biological treatment of abattoir, dairy and winery wastewater as something we could take on. That is a commercially valuable claim — but it is only strong when a specific project can be shown with it, along with the inlet wastewater characteristics, the pre-treatment, the technology used, the discharge requirements, the laboratory results and the time in service.

Until these are together, the correct wording is: special loads can be handled on the basis of individual engineering analysis and design. That is not a weaker claim, only a more precise one — and it does not give the impression that a standard plant is automatically suitable for any industrial wastewater.

Frequently asked questions

What we are asked most often

Can a working plant be visited?

That depends on the operator's consent, but on institutional and municipal projects it can often be arranged. Ask us — seeing a working plant says more than any document.

Do you have an industrial reference?

Write and tell us which industry, and we will look for a comparable project with laboratory results. If there is none we will say so — and then the project starts with a feasibility study, not with a reference.
