Load profile

The nominal headcount is only one figure. Design needs the wastewater's load profile over time and by quality — and that differs by type of facility.

What sizing can be based on

The nominal headcount is only one figure

For a larger system, the nominal headcount or bed count is not enough on its own. Design needs the wastewater's load profile over time and by quality is what is needed.

The profile contains at least these: the average daily volume of water, the maximum daily and short-period hydraulic peak, the operating days, the seasonal variation and the origin of the wastewater.

This is not bureaucracy. It shows whether the system can run stably under real operation — or only on average days.

It differs by type of facility

Which figure is decisive?

The same way of expressing capacity cannot serve a dwelling, a school, a restaurant and a plant alike.

  • 01

    A guesthouse, accommodation. The occupancy and the peak weekends determine the sizing situation — not the bed count.

  • 02

    School, institution. The daytime load window, the drop at weekends and the longer holidays. A strong daily rhythm with substantial periods of underload.

  • 03

    Campsite, holiday camp. The seasonal maximum and the near-zero load out of season. An extreme curve.

  • 04

    Restaurant, commercial kitchen. The kitchen's operating hours and the number of meals a day. Here the organic load can be the bottleneck, not the volume of water.

  • 05

    Plant, works. The shifts and the process water streams. The staff headcount applies only to the sanitary stream.

Two separate loads

They have to be measured separately and sized separately

Hydraulic — how much WATER arrives

  • Average m³/day
  • Maximum m³/day
  • Hourly or per-shift peak, where relevant
  • Read from the water meter; measured at an existing facility
  • The unit's throughput capacity is the constraint

Organic — how much MATTER has to be broken down

  • BOD5, COD and suspended solids
  • At a commercial kitchen or a plant it requires laboratory analysis
  • It cannot be read off the water meter
  • It can differ greatly at the same volume of water
  • The basis for sizing the biological stage

Data quality

Four levels, and all of them matter

Where there is no reliable figure, do not estimate — mark the uncertainty and start gathering data.

  • 01

    Measured. Water meter data, laboratory results, operating log. This can be relied on.

  • 02

    Documented. An earlier drawing, permit or occupancy statement. Good, but whether it is current has to be checked.

  • 03

    Estimated. The owner's knowledge and experience. Enough for pre-screening, not for sizing.

  • 04

    Unknown. Not a failing but a task: measurement, sampling or expert data gathering follows from it.

Next step

After the profile comes the pre-treatment

Once the load is assembled, the next question is whether anything is needed ahead of the biological stage — a grease trap, balancing, a pumping station.

Frequently asked questions

What we are asked most often

We have no water consumption figures; the facility has not been built yet.

For a new facility an estimate is needed: from the planned capacity, the operating pattern and data from comparable businesses. We mark the uncertainty separately in that case, and more headroom has to be built into the design.

Is a laboratory analysis needed?

For a purely domestic load, typically not a precondition. For a commercial kitchen, a significant grease load and any wastewater of process origin, yes — without it the sizing would rest on assumption.

What is a load curve?

How the load runs over time: how the daily volume of water varies across the week, the month and the year. It shows the spread around the average — and that is what determines how much headroom is needed.
