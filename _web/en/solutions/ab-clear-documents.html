Documents and certificates

Every technical claim has to lead to a document. That is the difference between a marketing claim and usable technical information.

What this page will be

A single, versioned body of evidence

The aim is for this page to be the single authoritative, up-to-date home for the A.B.Clear products' technical and conformity papers — searchable by model and document type.

Today this body of evidence is scattered: the CE and EN 12566-3 conformity, the accredited testing, the patent and the earlier permit examples all appear separately on the site, but they do not add up to a versioned document library.

This is one of the largest untapped opportunities for A.B.Clear's technical credibility — and, frankly, the easiest to put right.

Which documents belong here

The structure of the document library

Every paper has to carry: model, version, date of issue, validity status and document owner.

  • 01

    Declaration of performance and CE document. Per model, indicating the link to EN 12566-3.

  • 02

    Test report. The full accredited test document the outlet water values refer to.

  • 03

    Operating and maintenance manual. Per model, with the current version.

  • 04

    Installation guide. For the contractor and the client alike.

  • 05

    Technical data sheet and product drawing. Dimensions, levels, connections.

  • 06

    Warranty and guarantee. Together with the terms.

  • 07

    Szabadalmi document. For the sludge bag solution, with status by country.

  • 08

    Examples of earlier permits. Marked strictly as „example, historical document” — these do NOT prove that a permit would be granted automatically today.

Why versioning matters

Two separate needs

New sales

  • The current models need their current documents
  • The conformity reference has to point at the standard in force
  • The designer needs today's data sheet, not last year's
  • A valid document is needed for the regulatory procedure

Supporting older products

  • A unit installed ten years ago belongs with the manual OF THAT TIME
  • Supplying parts requires knowing the production version
  • The old document has to stay available — with archive status
  • The two must not be confused: anyone operating an old system should not be given today's data sheet

The chain of evidence

Claim → document → source

Every technical claim has to lead to a document, and every document has to be identifiable. If we write that the unit conforms to a standard, the conformity paper should be one click away.

This is not a formality. For the designer, the contractor and the regulator, this is precisely the difference between a marketing claim and usable technical information — and it is what competitors typically do not do.

In the meantime

Ask for what you need

Until the document library is assembled, we send the papers you need directly. Write and tell us which model and which document — and what you need it for, because that decides which version is the right one.

Frequently asked questions

What we are asked most often

Can I have the declaration of performance before buying?

Yes. Write and tell us which model interests you and we will send it. For design and for a permitting procedure it is often necessary anyway.

Where do I find documentation for my older unit?

Give us the type of unit and roughly the year of installation — we will find the documentation belonging to it from that time. Today's data sheet is not necessarily valid for a system installed ten years ago.

Do the earlier permit examples prove that it will be permitted?

No. Those are 2020 documents belonging to particular projects, and they do not mean a permit would be granted automatically today. They are useful as examples — they show what data such a procedure contained — but today's rules always have to be checked from a current source.
