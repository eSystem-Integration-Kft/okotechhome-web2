Models and capacities

Transparency about capacity — but not automatic model selection. The actual consumption, the peak load and the plot data are all part of the decision too.

What this page is for

Transparency about capacity

This page sets out the whole range in a single consistent data structure so that a designer and a contractor can work from it: nominal population equivalent, hydraulic capacity, range of application, tank size and every technical parameter that genuinely affects the choice of model.

What it does not give: automatic model selection. Transparency about capacity is not the same as a visitor picking the right unit alone, from a single figure.

What data is needed

These are needed for each model

This list is also the list of missing data — see the note below.

  • 01

    Nominal population equivalent (PE). The organic load capacity.

  • 02

    Maximum daily hydraulic capacity (m³/day). This is always needed alongside „persons”.

  • 03

    Inlet and outlet levels. These determine what pipe depth it can be installed at without a riser.

  • 04

    Dimensions and weight. For planning the excavation and the lift into place.

  • 05

    Nominal and peak load capability. Only with verified, documented figures — see below.

  • 06

    Underload range. How far below the nominal it runs stably.

  • 07

    Version and date. Which production variant the figure applies to.

On the overload figures

Why they do not appear in the table

Our current material contains two different overload claims: one says the unit withstands a sustained 30% overload, the other that for 2–3 days it can handle as much as 150%.

Both claims may well be true at once — they speak of different timescales. They can go into a capacity table only once it is documented what the percentage refers to (headcount, volume of water or organic load), which models, for how long, at what temperature és against what outlet water quality criterion.

Without these the percentage is too easily misread — and in sizing it is exactly this kind of figure that causes trouble later. So we would rather publish no number than one we cannot qualify.

When it leaves the standard band

Two boundaries

Standard model selection

  • Wastewater domestic in character
  • The load falls within the nominal range
  • The peaks are predictable and regular
  • The plot conditions are known
  • A catalogue model and the usual arrangement

Bespoke engineering route

  • A load above 50 PE
  • Non-domestic or process wastewater
  • A highly unpredictable load profile
  • A solution for several properties or part of a settlement
  • Bespoke design, a different permitting route

Next step

The profile is the input

Choosing a model is not the first step but the result of the load profile. Once that is known, the question of the model is clearer — and there is no need to size for the maximum.

Frequently asked questions

What we are asked most often

Why there is no capacity table here?

Because our public material currently has no consistent data sheet covering every model. We would rather say what is missing than publish incomplete or contradictory figures. On a specific project our engineering colleagues give exact figures.

Should we size for the maximum number of guests?

No. A larger system chosen for a peak that occurs a few times a year would be persistently underloaded on every other day — and that is an operating problem in its own right. The average and the regular peak together are the right starting point.
