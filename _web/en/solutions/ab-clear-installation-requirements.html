Installation requirements

These are not details but preconditions of the works. What we settle in advance costs nothing extra at the edge of the pit.

Why it has its own page

It should not emerge from the small print

The installation preconditions have until now been scattered: some among the FAQs, some in the small print of the order form. This page gathers them in one place — because they are not details but preconditions of the works.

The aim is that the project should not begin with surprises on site. What we settle here in advance costs nothing extra at the edge of the pit.

Preparing the site

What has to be provided?

  • 01

    The position of the tank. At an adequate distance from the building, the well and the plot boundary, planned together with access for servicing.

  • 02

    The level of the inlet pipe. This determines whether a riser element or a pumping chamber is needed. The exact per-model values need to be confirmed — see the note below.

  • 03

    Groundwater and anchoring the tank. In high groundwater a concrete surround or another anchored arrangement is needed against flotation.

  • 04

    The path of the treated water. The position and design of the soakaway or other disposal arrangement — a precondition of the project, not a later addition.

  • 05

    Electrical supply point. A properly protected electrical connection to the unit.

  • 06

    Vehicle loading. Vehicles cannot be driven over the tank without a separately sized concrete chamber.

  • 07

    Access for machinery. Access width, overhanging obstructions, the route for lowering it in, where the spoil goes.

  • 08

    Access for servicing. The cover has to remain openable and accessible for good.

Who carries it out

Division of responsibility

This may be the client or their own contractor

  • Earthworks, excavating the pit
  • Laying the pipework from the house
  • Building the electrical supply point
  • Building the disposal arrangement for the treated water
  • Backfill and reinstating the ground
  • Delivery — can also be arranged yourself

Available as an ÖkoTech service

  • Delivery to site
  • Installation
  • Commissioning and start-up
  • Handover and training the owner
  • These are separate service items, not automatically part of ordering the unit

Handover and training

What happens at the end of the works

Commissioning is not switching the unit on. It includes checking that the system works, setting the controller and — what is most often left out — the owner's training.

The training covers how to carry out the settlement test, how to handle the sludge bag, what the controller's indications mean and what has to be checked routinely. It is worth taking seriously — the system's long-term operation turns on it.

The handover is documented, and the papers belonging to the unit — operating manual, declaration of performance, guarantee — pass over at that point.

Next step

The plot data comes first

Most of the installation requirements are plot data. Once you have it, the works can be planned — if not, an on-site survey closes the open questions.

Frequently asked questions

What we are asked most often

Can it be installed by my own contractor?

The earthworks, pipework and electrical supply point can typically be carried out by your own contractor. Installation and commissioning are separate service items — we agree their terms with the specific order, because the guarantee can be tied to them.

How long does the installation take?

That depends on the earthworks and the conditions on site. Placing the unit is a short process in itself; the whole works — including building the disposal arrangement — typically takes several days. We can give an exact schedule on the specific project.

Is a permit required?

That depends on the project and the location, and the procedural rules change from time to time. Our earlier permit examples are from 2020 and cannot be used as today's procedural rules. The project preparation section deals with this, citing current sources.
