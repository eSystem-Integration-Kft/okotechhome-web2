Monitoring and operation

Handover is not the end of the project but the start of a regulated operating process. And this has to be known as early as the choice of technology.

Handover is not the end

At a larger treatment plant, handover is not the end of the project but the start of a the start of a regulated operating process. This has to be settled before the quote, because it affects the choice of technology too.

The responsible operator has to be named, along with the frequency of checks, the content of the operating log, the sampling and laboratory duties, how alarms are handled and the service response time.

The regulations do not treat this as optional either: the authority may check that the operating log is kept, that self-monitoring results conform, and that the discharge limits are observed — the detailed rules for sampling and analysis are set out in a separate decree.

Who does what

Division of responsibility

The on-site operator

  • Daily and weekly visual checks
  • Keeping the operating log
  • Tracking flow and load
  • First response and notification in the event of an alarm
  • Keeping the sampling point accessible
  • Managing consumables

ÖkoTech and the laboratory

  • Preventive maintenance and periodic inspection
  • Parts replacement, mechanical repair
  • Checking and setting the controller
  • Resolving escalated faults
  • Accredited laboratory: sampling and analysis
  • Evaluating the results and reporting

A regular feladatok

Daily, weekly, monthly, annual

The exact schedule depends on the project and follows what the permit prescribes. The structure, however, is similar at every larger plant.

  • 01

    Napi or heti. Visual check, checking the state of the controller, an entry in the operating log, tracking the incoming flow.

  • 02

    Monthly. A more detailed inspection, checking the consumables, collating the load data.

  • 03

    At the frequency prescribed in the permit. Sampling and laboratory analysis, comparing the results with the discharge requirements.

  • 04

    Annual. A full inspection, preventive maintenance, parts replacement, collating the documentation and reporting.

  • 05

    Ad hoc. Alarms, overload, a deviation in water quality — these need an escalation procedure set out in advance.

What has to be decided in advance

Before the quote, not after

  • 01

    Who the named responsible operator is, and with what competence

  • 02

    What training they receive, and when

  • 03

    Who covers for them during leave and holidays

  • 04

    What data is measured, and how often

  • 05

    Who takes the samples, and which laboratory analyses them

  • 06

    Who evaluates the results, and who they report to

  • 07

    What happens in the event of a fault — who is called, and within what time

  • 08

    Whether there is remote monitoring, and if so, who watches it

  • 09

    What service response time we undertake, and under what arrangement

  • 10

    Where and for how long the documentation is kept

The permit sets it too

The frequency and content of monitoring is partly prescribed by the permit. So the permitting route is worth looking at early too.

Frequently asked questions

What we are asked most often

Does it need a qualified operator?

The requirement depends on the project, and the permit may set it too. What is certain: a named person responsible is needed, with training, and the task is not a side activity. We agree the exact level of competence on the specific project.

Is there remote monitoring?

We are currently documenting the controller's capabilities and the remote monitoring option. On a specific project we will tell you what is available — precisely rather than in general terms.

How quickly does a service engineer come out?

We promise no specific response time here, because it is a contractual matter and depends on the arrangement. On a larger system this is exactly what is worth recording in the quote — ask, and we will agree it.

What happens if it deviates from the discharge limit?

An investigation of the cause follows: load, pre-treatment, the condition of the biology, a departure from the operating regime. The regulatory consequences are set by the permit and the legislation — which is why it matters that self-monitoring flags the deviation in good time.
