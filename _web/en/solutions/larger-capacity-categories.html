Capacity and project categories

Three different things tend to get run together: the legal category, the boundary of the product range, and the project's actual load.

Three separate categories

What our site has until now run together

Three different things tend to be spoken of as capacity, and they are not the same: the legal category, the boundary of ÖkoTech's product ranges, and the project's actual load.

The statutory boundary is clear: standalone treatment under Government Decree 147/2010 extends to at most 50 population equivalent. That is a genuine regulatory dividing line — above it a different design and permitting route follows.

The product boundary and the actual load, however, do not coincide with it, and at present our own communication is not consistent about this either. We say so here rather than papering over it.

The categories

Four bands, four different project routes

The exact figures for the boundaries will appear here after internal validation. The structure of the categories, however, is already usable.

  • 01

    Standard standalone range. A domestic-type load, a catalogue model, the usual arrangement. This is the legal category too, up to 50 PE.

  • 02

    Modular transitional range. Several standard units linked together. The load exceeds the standalone category, but the technology is still built from known elements.

  • 03

    Large-plant product range. A tank or containerised system built specifically for large capacity, with unified control.

  • 04

    Bespoke industrial route. Non-domestic wastewater. Here capacity does not categorise on its own — the technology and the laboratory data decide.

What changes when the boundary is crossed

Not only the size

Below 50 PE

  • The standalone treatment category under Decree 147/2010
  • A catalogue model can be chosen
  • A simpler design route
  • The owner may be the operator
  • The inspection and documentation duties are simpler

Above 50 PE

  • It leaves the standalone category
  • A qualified designer sizes it
  • A different permitting route, different documentation
  • A named responsible operator is required
  • Operating log, self-monitoring, and typically sampling too
  • The discharge requirements have to be examined separately

„Persons” as a figure for communication

How far it takes you

„How many people?” is user-friendly, and for a domestic-type, known pattern of use it is a good approximation. Most of the market starts there for a reason.

At a guesthouse, an institution or a plant, however, the bed count and the staff headcount particularly misleading: the same number of „persons” can mean an entirely different volume of water and organic load. There, categorisation has to rest on PE, m³/day, peak load and, where needed, laboratory data.

So we use „persons” as an entry figure and ask for others alongside it.

Next step

The category needs load data

The input to categorisation is the load profile: average, peak, seasonality and the origin of the wastewater. Without it the classification remains an estimate.

Frequently asked questions

What we are asked most often

Where exactly does the A.B.Clear range end?

We give no exact figure for this at present, because our own material is not consistent about it, and we would rather say we are reconciling it than publish an uncertain boundary. On a specific project our engineering colleagues give an exact answer.

What belongs to a 60-person institution?

That is exactly the band we are reconciling. A modular solution or the lower end of the large-plant route typically comes into play there — but the answer needs the actual load, not the headcount. Send us the water consumption and the operating profile and we will tell you.

Why is the bed count not enough?

Because the bed count shows the maximum, not the load. A twenty-bed guesthouse may run at an annual average of eight guests — sizing starts from the average and the regular peak, not from the nominal capacity.
