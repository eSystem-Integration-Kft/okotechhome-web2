Cost calculator

Emptying cost calculator

The cost of emptying differs from one settlement to the next, and it is rarely a single figure: a call-out charge, a per-cubic-metre emptying charge and, in many places, a minimum charge all appear on the bill. This calculator works out the annual cost from your own tariff — and shows how much you pay for a volume that is never taken away.

What this page gives you

What it shows — and what it does not

Emptying is not our service and not our price. This calculator exists so that, from your own tariff, it is clear what this costs each year and what it turns on.

What it shows

  • The per-visit and annual charge from the tariff you enter
  • What makes up the amount — item by item
  • How much you pay for a volume that is not taken away
  • How the unit rate changes if you empty less often, with a fuller tank
  • Where we have got to in building the settlement tariff database

What it cannot tell you

  • It is not a price list: we do not know every settlement's tariff, and we will not invent one
  • It does not replace the utility's quotation or the local authority's decree
  • It does not tell you how often to empty — the sludge level decides that
  • It does not calculate VAT: enter what appears on your bill
  • It sends nothing anywhere — the calculation runs in your browser, with no data leaving it

The formula

How it calculates

Five steps, every one of which can be checked on paper. The calculator contains no hidden multiplier and no “rule of thumb” correction.

  • 01

    Chargeable volume. The charge is not always based on what is actually taken away. Where the minimum charge includes a given volume, the chargeable volume is the greater.

  • 02

    Base charge. The emptying charge multiplied by the chargeable volume — but at least the minimum charge. The minimum charge is a floor: where the volume-based amount is lower, the minimum charge takes its place.

  • 03

    Call-out charge. Once per visit, regardless of volume. Not a separate item with every provider — in that case enter zero, and it does not appear in the cost bar at all.

  • 04

    Further items. A distance-based charge (HUF/km multiplied by the kilometres to be travelled) and any other per-visit item on your bill. Both can be left out.

  • 05

    Annual cost. The per-visit charge multiplied by the number of visits a year. You supply the frequency — the calculator does not estimate it, because it depends on the sludge level and on use, not on the tariff.

Database

Where we have got to with settlement charges

The emptying charge differs by settlement and by provider, and there is no public, consolidated register of it. We build this database from submissions — with rows evidenced by a bill, a provider's price table or a local authority decree, and nothing else. Until we have verified data for a settlement, it will not appear on the map.

The map is made of tiles, not boundaries: every county gets a field of the same size, and their positions follow the real east–west and north–south order. We deliberately draw no exact borders — we have no authoritative cartographic source for them.

Submitting data

Do you know the charges that apply in your settlement?

Send them in — and the next enquirer will find them ready. We only add a row that comes with a source, and we record every submission after a human check, so the data does not appear on the map at once. We ask for no name and no email address; give an email only if you would like us to come back to you.

Leave any item you do not know empty — an empty field means we do not know, whereas zero is a real value (no call- out charge, for instance). We do not confuse the two.

A *marked fields are required. A tariff is not personal data in itself; we ask for an email address only if you would like a response.

How much has to be emptied depends on the technology

This calculator takes the tariff apart — how much has to be removed, and how often, is decided by the system installed. With a sealed holding tank the whole of the wastewater is removed; with a septic tank system, the sludge; with an active biological unit the operating profile is different again. Frequently asked questions

What we are asked most often

Why do you not state the emptying charge for my settlement?

Because we do not know it. Collecting domestic wastewater not carried by mains is a public service: the charge is set by the utility and the local authority, separately for each settlement, and there is no public, consolidated register of it. And we will not publish an invented or “roughly this much” figure — that is worse than nothing, because a visitor would believe it. This is why the calculator asks for the charges, and why we collect them settlement by settlement.

What does it mean that the minimum charge includes x m³?

That in return for the minimum charge the provider undertakes to remove a given volume — and if you have less removed, you still pay for that much. Where this volume equals the tanker's capacity, you pay for the whole tanker even if a single cubic metre is taken. This is why “volume included in the minimum charge” is a separate field in the calculator: it can be anything from zero up to the tanker's capacity.

What if there is no call-out charge?

Then enter zero in the field. The call-out charge is not a separate item with every provider — with some it is built into the volume-based charge or the minimum charge. The calculator treats zero as a real value, and this slice then does not appear in the cost bar at all.

Should I enter gross or net values?

Whichever appears on your bill — the calculator does not compute VAT, it only adds and multiplies. On a household bill this is typically the gross figure. If you work with net charges, the result will be net too; but do not mix the two within one calculation.

How do I find out my settlement's tariff?

From three places: your latest emptying bill, the utility's price table, or the local authority's waste management or wastewater transport decree. If you have any of them, you can submit it through the form — and the next enquirer will see it on the map.

How often does it need emptying?

The calculator gives no answer to this, because it is not a question of tariffs: the sludge level governs, not the calendar, and the frequency is determined by the tank's size, the number of users and how intensively it is used. You supply the frequency in the calculator — we do not estimate it for you.
