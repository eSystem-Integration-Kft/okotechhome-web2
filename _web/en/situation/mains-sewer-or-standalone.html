Mains sewer or a standalone system?

This question has to be settled BEFORE the technology is chosen. It is not a matter of opinion, and the pipe in the street does not decide it — official information does. In the reverse order, you find out mid-project that it cannot be built.

This page touches on a legal question

The information below is general, and the regulations may change. For the answer that applies to your own property from the regional water utility and the competent local authority is what you have to obtain — no website substitutes for that, ours included.

Legal content last reviewed by a specialist: 9 August 2026 The review was based on the text available in the National Legislation Register, Government Decree 147/2010 (IV. 29.) as currently in force.

The legal framework

Two conditions the decree names

Under Government Decree 147/2010 (IV. 29.), what carries particular weight is whether, on the public land bordering the property, the sewerage works are whether it is technically available, and whether there is adequate treatment plant capacity.

If both conditions are met

Under the decree no new wastewater treatment unit may be installed. This is not a matter of preference and price does not decide it: connection is the route. We will say so even when it means you will not be buying from us.

If either is not met

The next question opens up: which on-site solution suits the property? That does not yet mean a biological system, a septic tank or any particular unit will be right — the conditions of the plot and the use have to be examined next.

Environmental conditions affect the installation requirements too: the regulations treat highly sensitive with regard to subsurface waters, and in high groundwater areas separately. In certain cases a municipal wastewater programme, a water base protection condition or further investigation may come into play — so for a specific property, information from the local authority, a designer or the authority may also be needed.

The real question

It is not whether there is a pipe in the street

In everyday speech, „there is a sewer” means a manhole is visible nearby. The regulations, however, work with a different concept: the technical availability of the sewerage works and adequate treatment plant capacity count together.

The distinction has a practical consequence: if both hold, installing a new standalone treatment unit is not a viable route — it is not a matter of preference. If they do not, the standalone route is open.

Hence the right order: first establish the mains sewer position, then choose the technology. The other way round, you find out mid-project that it cannot be built.

What you see first, and what actually has to be established
What you see first What is actually worth establishing
Is there a pipe in the street? Is it technically available for the property?
Is the neighbour connected? What conditions apply to your property?
Does the neighbour have their own system? When was it built, and on what conditions?
Does the sewer seem far away? What is the official technical connection position?

The example of a neighbouring property, a utility map, or where the pipe visibly runs therefore gives no reliable answer on its own for your project.

What to check

Four questions, and who to ask

You need official answers to these. The neighbour's experience and the wording of a listing are not enough.

  • 01

    Are the sewerage works technically available on the public land bordering the property? Ask: the regional water utility.

  • 02

    Is there adequate treatment plant capacity? This can be a constraint even when the pipe is physically there. Ask: the utility.

  • 03

    Is the network planned, and when? Ask for a binding schedule, not verbal information. Ask: the local authority.

  • 04

    Does a water base protection or other restriction apply to the area? This can override every other consideration. Ask: the competent authority or the local council.

Results

Four outcomes — every one of them valid

  • 01

    The mains sewer is not available. The standalone route is open; the next task is to gather the plot and load data.

  • 02

    The mains sewer is available. Connection is then the route. We will say so even when it means you will not be buying from us.

  • 03

    Construction is scheduled and the date is known. It is worth comparing the connection and service charges with the full life-cycle cost of a standalone system. Waiting is a legitimate decision.

  • 04

    There is no clear answer. This is the most common outcome. The next task is then to obtain official information — and that we can help with.

Cost

What is worth comparing — without a specific price

  • 01

    On the mains sewer side: the connection fee, building the pipe run within the plot, and the ongoing service charge.

  • 02

    On the standalone system side: the investment, earthworks, energy, sludge handling and maintenance.

  • 03

    The time horizon decides it. The service charge runs for decades; with a standalone system it is avoided, but an operating duty remains in its place. The two sides can only be compared over the same period.

The order

Two gates, not one

Settling the mains sewer position is only the first gate. If the standalone route is open, it does not yet follow which system you need.

  • 01

    Can standalone treatment be considered at all? Official information from the water utility and the local authority answers this — on the basis of technical availability and treatment plant capacity.

  • 02

    Which standalone solution suits the project? Here what decides is permanent or intermittent use, the expected occupancy and load, the free area of the plot, the soil, the groundwater and the disposal of the treated water. The decree itself names the characteristics of the soil, the depth of the subsurface water and the quantity of wastewater in connection with sizing and construction.

While that data is still missing, choosing a specific unit would be premature.

Cost

The two routes build their costs differently

„Which is cheaper?” cannot be answered reliably in general without knowing the property's data. What makes the comparison meaningful is working through the same items on both routes.

What belongs to one route and what to the other
Mains sewer route Standalone system route
Connection Unit or system
Construction work required Earthworks and installation
Arrangement within the plot Building the disposal arrangement for the treated water
Long-term service charges Energy, emptying or sludge handling, maintenance, servicing

In both cases the actual costs are affected considerably by the particular property and the technical arrangement it requires. So it is worth asserting neither in general that „the mains sewer is cheaper” nor that „you are better off with your own system”.

Frequently asked questions

What is not worth deciding on assumption

If there is a sewer in the street, is connection compulsory?

Without knowing the property's current technical and legal position, a general yes or no is not worth giving. What has to be checked is the actual availability of the mains sewer and the conditions attached — with the utility and the local authority.

Can I have my own treatment unit if there is a sewer in the street?

That there is a pipe in the street does not give the whole answer on its own. For the regulations, the technical availability és a adequate treatment plant capacity matters too: if both hold, installing a new unit is not a viable route.

What if the sewer is built later?

It is worth checking how firm and how documented the development is, and how it fits the timing of your build or your use of the property. A general development plan or a verbal promise does not carry the same certainty as a documented, scheduled project.

What if the sewer is too far away?

„Too far” is everyday language, not a technical decision criterion. The actual possibility of connection is worth judging from official or utility information, not from how far you can see.
