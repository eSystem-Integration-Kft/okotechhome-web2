Which data should be gathered first?

This list is not an exam. For each figure it says why it matters, where it can be found, and whether it may be estimated or has to be measured. If you do not know something, that is not a failure — it shows what the next step is.

How you use it

„I do not know” is a valid answer too

This list is not an exam. In the tables below, each figure carries why it matters, where it can be found, and that can be estimated-e or must be measured.

If you do not know something, that is not a failure but information: it shows that the next step is obtaining a document, an on-site survey or bringing in an expert. An estimated figure, one taken from a document and one actually measured are not equally reliable — and so they suffice for different things.

To get your bearings, the name of the municipality, how the property is used and the occupancy are enough. The rest becomes necessary at the sizing and quotation stage.

Data quality

Not every figure is equally certain — note where you know it from

The same number carries quite different weight depending on whether it was estimated from memory, appears on a drawing, or was actually measured. So it is worth noting the source beside every important figure — that is what shows what it suffices for.

  • 01

    Estimated. Enough for a first orientation on certain figures — the frequency of use or the approximate size of the free area already shows which questions have to be settled. Which estimates can be used for a later technical decision has to be decided separately.

  • 02

    Documented. Taken from a drawing, a utility statement or another document. Say what kind of document it is and how current — a statement issued ten years ago is not the same as last year's.

  • 03

    Measured. An actual measurement. For certain decisions — typically on groundwater and infiltration capacity — only this is acceptable; exactly where, is decided by the project and the technical question at hand.

  • 04

    Not known. Not an obstacle in itself. It helps us start from something other than an uncertain estimate and find the right next step instead. This answer moves things forward too.

The distinction matters especially for groundwater, soil, pipe depth and the documents available. Where an uncertain estimate produces a technical conclusion that looks certain, the site will refute it later — typically at the most expensive moment.

Frequently asked questions

What we are asked most often

What if I do not know the groundwater?

This is the most commonly missing figure, and it is no obstacle to getting your bearings. The design groundwater level can be established by measurement; if a geotechnical report is being prepared for the build anyway, it is worth asking for the wastewater aspects to be included — one investigation, answers to two questions.

How much data is worth gathering before I contact you?

You do not have to wait until you have everything. The name of the municipality, how the property is used and the occupancy are enough for us to say which directions are in play. The detailed figures are needed at the sizing stage.

Is estimated data enough for a quote?

For an order-of-magnitude indication yes, for a final quote no. The installation circumstances — earthworks, connection depth, water disposal — drive the total most, and these have to be measured, not estimated.

I do not know how deep the wastewater pipe leaves the building.

It is worth looking at the drawings or the construction documentation first. If the figure is not there either, measurement may be needed later. It is not for you to decide whether pumping is required, for instance — giving the underlying figure accurately is enough.

I have no site plan.

That does not in itself mean you cannot go on. For a first round a hand-drawn sketch helps, provided it shows the building, the plot boundary, the free area and the position of the well. An accurate drawing becomes necessary when the sizing and the siting become concrete.

I do not know the water consumption.

On a new build there is often no such figure — the occupancy and the pattern of use are then the starting point. If you do not know the consumption, write that it is unknown: a number given at random is worse than a missing one.

When does a specialist have to be involved?

Not every unknown calls immediately for a surveyor, a hydrogeologist or a designer. It is worth establishing first which information is missing and which decision it affects — after that it can be decided whether further measurement or an expert is needed. We have a separate page on this: when an expert is needed.
