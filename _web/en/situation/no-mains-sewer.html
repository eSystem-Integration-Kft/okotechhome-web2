No mains sewer available

For a proportion of occupied properties in Hungary there is no mains sewer, and there will not be one within any foreseeable time. The wastewater then has to be dealt with within the plot — but how matters. This page works through which solutions are in play, when each drops out, and what has to be established for them.

Options

What options are there?

There are four directions. What decides the choice first is not the price but where the treated water can go and what the local regulations allow.

  • 01

    Biological wastewater treatment unit. It treats the wastewater within the plot, and the treated water can be soaked away or — where the conditions are met — put to use. For permanent occupation this is the most common solution. It needs electricity, and regular but light operating attention.

  • 02

    Septic tank system with a soakaway. A simpler solution with no power requirement. It only works where the permeability of the soil is adequate and the groundwater sits deep enough — and the soakaway field takes up a considerable area.

  • 03

    Sealed holding tank. It does not treat, only collects: the wastewater has to be taken away regularly. Its place is where local conditions allow nothing at all to enter the ground. We do not manufacture this solution, but we will say so when that is the situation.

  • 04

    Waiting for the mains sewer. If the local authority has a firm, scheduled development plan and a connection is available within a foreseeable time, waiting with an interim solution may be worthwhile. A promise and a schedule, however, are not the same thing.

Weighing it up

Mains sewer or a standalone system?

It tips towards a standalone system if…

  • there is no firm development schedule — the local authority cannot name a binding date;
  • the connection point is far away, and the cost of the pipe run within the plot is significant on its own;
  • the plot is suitable for disposing of the treated water, so the system can be closed off within the plot;
  • you are planning for the long term: over the life of the system the service charge is avoided as well.

It tips towards the mains sewer if…

  • the network is being built and the connection date is known — a parallel investment is hard to justify in that case;
  • the plot allows nothing to enter the ground, and there is no other way of disposing of the treated water;
  • the main runs immediately next to the plot boundary, so the connection is technically straightforward;
  • you intend to sell the property in the short term — there, the utility connection is itself an asset.

Preparation

Which data should be gathered first?

With these answers we can already say which solutions are in play and which drop out — all before anyone visits the site.

  • 01

    The exact location of the property and the local regulations. The name of the municipality and the land registry number are enough to establish whether a water base protection or other restriction applies to the area.

  • 02

    How it is used, and by how many. Permanent occupation or intermittent use, how many people, and whether the load has a predictable peak (weekend guests, for instance).

  • 03

    The characteristics of the plot. Area, slope, the position of the built-up part, and where there is a free surface reachable by vehicle for the unit.

  • 04

    Soil and groundwater. An earlier geotechnical report or a drilled well data sheet helps a great deal. Failing that, the experience of neighbouring properties is a guide too.

  • 05

    What is intended to happen to the treated water. Whether there is a surface receiving water nearby (a ditch, a stream), whether you plan to reuse it for irrigation, and where the surrounding dug or drilled wells are.

Missing data

It is no problem if you do not yet have every figure

Most enquirers do not arrive already knowing the soil type, the groundwater situation, the depth of the pipe exit and the possible means of disposal. Nor do they need to. „I do not know” is not a wrong answer — it marks a piece of information still to be obtained.

If you do not know this yet, here is where it can be found
If you do not know this yet Where can it be found? What should the next step be?
What is the soil like? From an existing document or an on-site investigation Pinning the figure down
Where is the groundwater? From available data or from measurement Obtaining reliable information
Is the mains sewer actually available? From the utility or from local information Clarifying the situation
Where can the treated water go? From examining the plot conditions and the technical requirements Narrowing the options

Good preparation does not mean you have to design the system. It is enough to know which questions remain to be settled.

Check

Settle these six things before you choose a system

The first decision check needs no lengthy technical documentation. Start with these six questions.

  • Is it settled that for the property no suitable mains sewer is available?
  • Permanently or only intermittently is the property used?
  • How many people normally use it, and what is the maximum load likely to be?
  • How much usable area is available on the plot?
  • What do you currently know about the soil and the groundwater?
  • Is there any idea how to solve the onward disposal of the treated water?

If several of these questions are still unanswered, that is no obstacle. They are precisely what shows which information is worth obtaining next. If, on the other hand, most of them are settled, comparing the solutions can become considerably more concrete.

Cost

The real cost does not end with the price of the unit

„What does it cost?” is a fair question. But the prices of two different systems can only be compared meaningfully if we look beyond the moment of purchase.

  • 01

    The unit or system capital cost.

  • 02

    Installation and the earthworks required.

  • 03

    Any energy consumption — this depends on the technology.

  • 04

    Emptying or sludge handling according to how the system operates.

  • 05

    Routine maintenance.

  • 06

    Servicing needed later on.

Their weight differs from solution to solution and from project to project. A lower opening figure therefore does not necessarily mean the better choice over the longer term. Specific prices are only worth comparing once the actual project data is known — guessed or generic figures found online lead easily to the wrong conclusion.

Pitfalls

Typical mistakes in this situation

  • 01

    Choosing the unit before examining the plot. The most common ordering mistake. Even a good unit is unworkable if there is nowhere for the treated water to go.

  • 02

    Waiting on a promise. „It will be built within a few years” — without a schedule and funding this is not a figure you can plan on. Meanwhile the cost of emptying runs on.

  • 03

    Ignoring the neighbouring wells. Protective distances apply not only to your own abstraction points but to those on neighbouring properties too.

  • 04

    Counting only the capital cost. The full picture only comes together with operation: electricity consumption, sludge removal, maintenance — without these the solutions cannot be compared.

Frequently asked questions

What we are asked most often

Is connection to the mains sewer compulsory once it is built?

A rákötési kötelezettség és annak határideje helyi rendelet kérdése, és településenként eltér. Ezt az illetékes önkormányzatnál vagy a szolgáltatónál érdemes tisztázni még a beruházás megkezdése előtt, mert befolyásolja a megtérülési számítást.

How long does installing a standalone system take?

The installation itself usually takes a few days, but the overall lead time is governed by permitting and earthworks. We can give a realistic schedule after the survey, because the depth of the connection and the ground conditions affect it considerably.

What happens if the sewer is built later after all?

A telepített rendszer nem válik értéktelenné: a kezelt víz hasznosítása és az elmaradó szolgáltatási díj továbbra is előny lehet. A rákötés és a meglévő rendszer sorsa azonban helyi szabályozás kérdése, ezért ezt előre érdemes tisztázni.

Does a treatment unit smell?

A szag kérdésére nem érdemes minden körülmény között érvényes ígéretet tenni. A megfelelően kiválasztott, telepített és üzemeltetett rendszer működését több tényező együtt határozza meg. Ha tartós, rendellenes szag jelentkezik, annak okát meg kell vizsgálni — az nem a technológia velejárója, hanem jelzés.

Does it need emptying?

That depends on the solution chosen and on how it works. With a sealed holding tank, removal is a fundamental part of operation; with other systems, sludge handling and emptying work differently. A precise frequency is only worth giving on the basis of verified system data and actual conditions of use.

Does it need electricity?

Not every wastewater solution works on the same principle. With active systems the energy demand is an important consideration; with other arrangements it may be different.

What happens during a power cut?

The answer depends on the system, so it has to be settled when the specific unit is chosen. Applying one general rule to every technology is not worth doing.

Terms

A few terms worth keeping apart

A szakkifejezések fontosak, de önmagukban nem segítenek dönteni. A lényeg az, hogy mit jelentenek az Ön ingatlana szempontjából.

Sealed holding tank
It collects the wastewater produced; the accumulated volume has to be taken away. It does not treat, only collect.
Septic tank system
Az oldómedence és a hozzá kapcsolódó további kezelési vagy elhelyezési megoldás together is how it should be understood. It is not simply a sealed tank under another name.
Active biological treatment unit
A system for treating wastewater on site, whose selection has to take account of both the load and the operating conditions.
Drainage field
Az oldómedencés rendszerhez kapcsolódó terület, amely a rendszer egészének működésében szerepet játszik — nem kiegészítő, hanem a technológia része.
Standalone wastewater treatment
An umbrella term for on-site solutions outside the public sewer network. The specific technical and legal categories always have to be settled for the project at hand.
