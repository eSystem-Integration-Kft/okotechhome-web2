What are you looking to solve?

The question of wastewater treatment rarely arrives on its own — it is always tied to a particular situation. Seven typical situations are set out below. Pick the one closest to yours: in each we begin with what has to be settled first, and only then comes the solution.

Situations

Which comes closest to your situation?

The situations can overlap. If two of them fit you, start with the one that comes first in time — the questions about the plot, for instance, come before choosing the unit.

  • No mains sewer available

    The property cannot be connected to the utility network, and it is unclear which solutions are in play at all.

    No mains sewer→

  • I am about to buy a plot or build

    Wastewater treatment turns on the suitability of the plot. That is worth establishing before buying or designing, not after.

    Plot and new build→

  • I want to replace an existing cesspit

    The cost of emptying is rising, or the system does not comply. Replacement is warranted only where the survey supports it.

    Replacing a cesspit→

  • Holiday or seasonally used property

    A long absence places different demands than permanent occupation. Not every technology takes it well.

    Holiday, seasonal→

  • I am looking for a system for a family home

    The house and the size of the household are given; the question is the solution type, the capacity and whether it can be installed.

    Family home→

  • I am looking for a solution for a business or institution

    Guesthouse, restaurant, school, campsite or plant: here the peak load and the composition of the wastewater decide, not the headcount on its own.

    Business, institution→

  • I already have a system and need help

    An operating question, a fault, a part or a service call: these belong to the Customer support section.

What they share

What is the same in all seven situations

Whichever route you start on, the same four questions decide what can be built. That is why we ask for the same data every time.

  • 01

    How much wastewater is produced, and at what rate. It is not the size of the house that matters but the actual load and how it fluctuates — permanent and intermittent use are two different tasks.

  • 02

    Where the treated water goes. In most projects this is the bottleneck: infiltration into the ground, a surface receiving water, or reuse for irrigation — each has different conditions.

  • 03

    What the plot allows. Soil structure, groundwater level, slope, existing development, existing utilities and the position of neighbouring wells together narrow the options.

  • 04

    What permitting conditions apply to the area. This differs by municipality and can override every other consideration — in a water base protection zone, for instance.

You cannot find yours

If none of the situations fits exactly

We have gathered the situations that recur across the projects so far, but the list is not exhaustive. For the more complex cases — several buildings, mixed use, an existing and a new system together — there is no template; they need a survey.

Describe your situation in a few sentences and we will tell you which direction is in play — including when the answer is that our system is not the solution.

Frequently asked questions

What we are asked most often

Which situation should I start with if several fit me?

With the one that comes first in time. The suitability of the plot, for instance, comes before choosing the unit: if the plot allows nothing to enter the ground, half the solution types drop out from the start.

Do I have to gather every figure in advance?

No. To get your bearings, the name of the municipality, how the property is used and the size of the household are enough. The detailed figures are only needed at the sizing and quotation stage.

Can you say in advance what it will cost?

An order of magnitude yes, a final price no. The total is driven mainly by the installation circumstances — the earthworks, the depth of the connection and the method of disposing of the treated water. These cannot responsibly be stated without an on-site survey.
