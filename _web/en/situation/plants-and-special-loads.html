Plants and special loads

The wastewater of office staff and wastewater from a production process are not the same task — even when the headcount is the same.

„Plant” is not a sizing category

Treating the domestic wastewater of office staff and treating wastewater from a production process are entirely different projects — even when the headcount is the same.

The regulations too treat used water arising from production and service activities as a wastewater category in its own right, and may set discharge limits specific to the industry and the pollutant. So the composition of the wastewater cannot be derived from headcount or from the daily volume of water.

The first step

Separate the two water streams

This is the single most important decision on this page — and in many cases the cheapest solution too.

  • 01

    Sanitary, domestic stream. The staff washroom, changing room and kitchen. Domestic in composition, it can be treated in the usual way.

  • 02

    Process stream. Water from production, washing and rinsing. Its composition is set by the technology, and it typically requires pre-treatment.

  • 03

    Why separation is worth it. The domestic stream is straightforward to treat; the process stream receives its own pre-treatment, sized accordingly. Treated together, the whole volume has to be designed to the stricter requirement.

What you need to provide

The process data sheet

  • 01

    The activity carried out on the site. What is made, and by what processes.

  • 02

    Raw and ancillary materials. What may enter the water.

  • 03

    Number of shifts and operating days per year.

  • 04

    Seasonality. A winery produces something entirely different at harvest than in winter — seasonality is what sets the peak load.

  • 05

    Daily and maximum volume of water. Average and peak m³/day, and the hourly or per-shift peak.

  • 06

    Process wash and rinse waters. When, how much, of what composition.

  • 07

    Chemicals in use. Cleaning agents, disinfectants and process chemicals.

  • 08

    Wastewater temperature. Where relevant — it affects the biological process.

  • 09

    Laboratory results. BOD5, COD, suspended solids; depending on the industry, fats and oils, nitrogen, phosphorus, pH and other components. The exact list is set by the technology.

  • 10

    Existing pre-treatment. Screen, grease trap, settling tank, balancing tank.

What you will not get from this page

And why not

There is no capacity table here and no model recommendation. Where there is no representative laboratory data, the website does not attempt an automatic system recommendation — the natural next step is sampling and an expert feasibility study.

Our earlier material mentioned the biological treatment of abattoir, dairy and winery wastewater as achievable. That professional capability may well exist, but no automatic sizing can be built on it on a website: the inlet water quality and the pre-treatment required differ from project to project.

Until there is a project we can show with its inlet wastewater characteristics, pre-treatment, technology, discharge requirements, laboratory results and operating history, the correct wording is this: special loads can be handled on the basis of individual engineering analysis and design.

This is not timidity. A badly sized industrial system does not waste the investor's money first — it makes meeting the discharge requirements impossible, and that is considerably harder to correct after the fact.

Next step

Let us start with the technology

Write and tell us what the activity is, over how many shifts, in what annual rhythm, and whether there are already laboratory results. From that we can say whether the existing data is sufficient or whether sampling has to come first.

Frequently asked questions

What we are asked most often

We have no laboratory results. Can we still request a quote?

Not a quote, responsibly, but a feasibility discussion yes. From the technology, the working pattern and the flow we can say what analysis is needed and what order of magnitude is involved. For sizing, laboratory data is unavoidable.

Which laboratory parameters are needed?

The minimum is typically BOD5, COD and suspended solids; beyond that, depending on the industry, fats and oils, nitrogen, phosphorus, pH and temperature. The exact list is set by the technology — which is why we ask about the production process first.

Is there anything you will not take on?

Yes, and we say so in advance. There are process wastewaters that cannot be treated biologically, or only with substantial pre-treatment. Where that is the case we say so during the feasibility study — not after the quote.

Can the staff wastewater be treated separately?

In many plants yes, and it is often the cheapest solution. It is worth examining at the design stage, because separating the network afterwards is substantial work.
