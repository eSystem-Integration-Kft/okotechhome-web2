Vehicle loading and access

Two separate questions: can the machine get in for the installation, and does access remain for servicing. The second is what is most often left out — and what causes trouble years later.

Two separate kinds of access

Installation and operation need different things

One question is how the machinery needed for installation gets onto the plot, and how the tank is placed. This is one-off, but on installation day there is no alternative to it.

The other is how the system stays reachable during use and servicing. That is a lasting requirement: the cover must remain openable and the unit inspectable and maintainable — years later too.

The most important constraint

No vehicle traffic over the tank

Over a standard plastic tank, vehicle traffic cannot run directly, and a road or pavement cannot simply be built over it. This is a product-specific structural limit, not timidity.

Where vehicle loading is unavoidable — because the only room is under the driveway, for instance — a separate, properly sized concrete chamber and prior technical consultation are required. This can be done, but it is a design decision and a cost item, not an afterthought.

The same applies to the area above the soakaway: there, compaction and paving impair the operation rather than endanger the structure — but the consequence is a failure just the same.

Access for installation

What to check before construction

Most of these can be settled in advance from photographs, without a site visit.

  • 01

    Gate width and height. The narrowest point of the drive, the actual dimension between the gate posts.

  • 02

    The drive's load-bearing capacity. On soft ground, freshly made ground or beside an embankment, the machine may not get in.

  • 03

    Overhanging obstacles. Overhead power lines, branches, a canopy — height constrains just as width does.

  • 04

    The route for lifting the tank in. The tank does not roll: it has to be lifted into place. Where the machine will stand and the route for the lift have to be settled in advance.

  • 05

    Where the excavated soil goes. The soil out of the excavation takes up a considerable volume. With nowhere to put it, it has to be carted away — a cost and extra traffic.

  • 06

    Planting and paving to be retained. What must not be disturbed, and what can be reinstated afterwards.

Access for servicing

What is needed for the long term

This is the part most often left out of the design — and it causes the most irritation years later.

  • 01

    The cover should open freely: no paving, flower bed, stored material or parked car over it

  • 02

    There should be room enough around the unit to work

  • 03

    The area over the soakaway should remain inspectable

  • 04

    The power supply and the controls should be accessible

  • 05

    Where emptying is needed, it should be reachable by a lorry

  • 06

    Planting should not encroach: a deep-rooted tree near the cover will cause trouble later

Frequently asked questions

What we are asked most often

Can the tank go under the driveway?

Only with a separate, properly sized concrete chamber and prior technical consultation. Vehicle traffic cannot run directly over a standard plastic tank. If there is no other room, this has to be settled at the design stage, because it carries consequences for cost and construction.

What happens if paving is later built over the cover?

The cover has to remain openable and accessible, or inspection and maintenance become impossible. If you are planning paving, say so in advance — the siting will then be different from the start, or the paving will have an openable section.

Can access be pre-screened from photographs?

Most questions, yes. The narrowest point of the drive, the gate, overhanging cables, the intended position and its surroundings can be judged well from photographs. If uncertainty remains after that, an on-site survey is warranted.
