Soakaway infiltration

Biological treatment has already happened inside the unit — the soakaway's job is to dispose of the water leaving it. Sizing turns on the daily volume and on the soil's MEASURED capacity to accept water.

What it means

Disposing of water already treated

With an active biological system, infiltration is not the main process of treatmentbut the placing of already biologically treated water into the soil. The distinction matters: here the soakaway does not treat, it receives.

The arrangement is below ground: the water leaving the unit reaches the soil through a distribution system, typically with elements bedded in gravel. Nothing of it shows at the surface, and the area remains usable — within certain limits.

Sizing is the critical point

It is not to be fitted to the space left over

Sizing the system properly is critical. With an undersized soakaway, backing up and the silting up of the soil can appear, leading to operating problems and costly remediation. This is not a theoretical risk but the most common after-the-fact fault in this type of system.

The size required does not follow from the type of unit but from four figures: the daily volume, the soil's measured infiltration capacity, a groundwater situation, and the available area. The volume of wastewater arriving appears, in essence, as the volume the system has to dispose of.

From this follows the most important practical rule: the soakaway cannot be sized to the space left in the yard. If the area required does not fit, the answer is not to make the soakaway smaller — another route for disposal has to be found.

The conditions

What has to be settled for infiltration

  • 01

    Measured infiltration capacity. At the soakaway's intended position és mélységében, szivárogtatási vizsgálattal. Talajtípus-megnevezésből nem vezethető le.

  • 02

    The highest seasonal groundwater. Nem a mai vízállás. At the soakaway's és a groundwater közötti viszony a működés feltétele.

  • 03

    The daily volume to be disposed of. It follows from the household's load; a greater load needs a proportionately larger area.

  • 04

    Enough free area. A szükséges felület, a szervizhozzáféréssel és a later területhasználattal together.

  • 05

    Gravitációs or szivattyús vízvezetés. If the soakaway sits higher than the unit's outlet, a pump is needed.

  • 06

    Wells, water base protection, sensitivity classification. From a water protection standpoint, the point at which treated water enters the ground is an object in its own right.

In the long run

What ruins it, and what keeps it working

These cause trouble

  • An undersized area — backing up, silting
  • Seasonal groundwater rising to the level of the soakaway
  • Compacting, paving or driving over the area
  • Deep-rooted planting above the soakaway
  • Neglected maintenance of the unit — the quality of the water leaving it deteriorates
  • Directing rainwater into the system

These keep it working

  • Sizing based on measured data, with a margin
  • A disposal level kept at an adequate distance from the groundwater
  • A terület fölött gyep or sekély gyökérzetű növényzet
  • Regular maintenance of the unit
  • Draining rainwater separately
  • Settling the area's future use in advance

Frequently asked questions

What we are asked most often

How large a soakaway does a family of four need?

We deliberately give no figure, because without the soil's measured infiltration capacity it would have no basis. The same household of four needs a far smaller area on one soil and several times as much on another. The occupancy is only one input — the measurement is the other.

Does anything show at the surface?

Jellemzően nem. A kialakítás felszín alatti, fölötte gyep or sekély gyökerű növényzet lehet. Ami nem lehet: burkolat, tömörítés, járműforgalom és mély gyökérzetű fa.

What happens if the soakaway is undersized?

The water does not leave quickly enough, it backs up, the soil's pores silt up, and the system deteriorates step by step. Remediation usually means breaking out and rebuilding the soakaway — considerably more expensive than the measurement beforehand would have been.

Can water be infiltrated in clay soil?

With more difficulty, over a larger area, and in some places not at all. Measurement decides this, not the name of the soil. Where on-site disposal cannot be achieved, another route has to be examined — and it is worth turning to a specialist early.
