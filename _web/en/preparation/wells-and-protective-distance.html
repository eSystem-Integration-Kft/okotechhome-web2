Wells and protective distance

It would be convenient to give a single protective distance. That would be misleading: the answer depends on the type of well, on the water base protection situation, and on where the water is actually disposed of.

The first thing to settle

The tank and the soakaway are two separate objects

From a water protection standpoint, the sealed treatment tank helye és az a pont, ahol a tisztított or előkezelt víz actually enters the ground. The tank is a sealed structure; the soakaway, by contrast, is precisely the object where water enters the geological medium. The question about the well concerns the latter above all.

This distinction appears in ÖkoTech's current guidance too, and it is a sound starting point. It does not follow, however, that the same holds for every type of well and every area classification.

Why we give no single figure

There is no universal answer to “how many metres?”

It would be convenient to give a single protective distance. That would be misleading, because the answer depends on several circumstances at once: the well's type (dug, bored, deep), legal status, a the water base protection situation, the direction of flow of the subsurface water, the groundwater level, and the position of the actual point of disposal.

For highly sensitive areas, Government Decree 147/2010 expressly ties the possibility of infiltrating treated wastewater to the water base protection rules, and Government Decree 123/1997 assigns protective zones and areas to water bases. These do not amount to a single figure in metres but to a site-dependent geometry.

Ha valaki egyetlen általános számot mond, az or egy konkrét helyzetre vonatkozik, or nem elég pontos. Ezért itt inkább azt mutatjuk meg, milyen adatokból áll össze a válasz.

What you need to provide

What data is needed about the well?

The more of these are known, the fewer expert rounds are needed. Both your own wells and the neighbours' have to be accounted for.

  • 01

    The type of well. Ásott, fúrt or mélységi. Az ásott kút jellemzően a talajvizet csapolja meg, a mélyebb fúrt kút más vízadó réteget — a kockázati kép ezért eltérő.

  • 02

    Its use. Ivóvíz, öntözés, állatitatás or nem használt. Az ivóvízcélú használat lényegesen szigorúbb megítélés alá esik.

  • 03

    Its exact position. Relative to the plot boundary and the intended soakaway, marked on a site plan. Including wells on neighbouring plots.

  • 04

    Its depth and strata, if known. For a bored well the well documentation may contain them. It shows which aquifer it reaches.

  • 05

    Documentation and legal status. Van-e engedélye or bejelentése. Ez nem a szennyvízprojekt kérdése, de a szakértői megítélést befolyásolja.

  • 06

    A designated water base protection area. Érint-e a telket védőidom or védőövezet. Ezt hivatalos forrásból kell ellenőrizni, nem becsülni.

The result

Four possible outcomes

  • 01

    No obvious conflict. On the data known, the intended arrangement meets no visible obstacle. This is not an official certificate but the result of pre-screening.

  • 02

    Further well data is needed. A kút típusa, mélysége or dokumentációja hiányzik. Ezek nélkül a megítélés nem megalapozott.

  • 03

    A water base protection check is needed. A telek kijelölt védőterületet érinthet, or a besorolás bizonytalan. Hivatalos forrásból kell tisztázni a szikkasztás megengedettségét.

  • 04

    The soakaway's position has to be changed. Az elrendezés átalakítható úgy, hogy az elhelyezési pont távolabb, más irányba or a felszín alatti víz áramlásához képest kedvezőbb helyzetbe kerüljön.

Frequently asked questions

What we are asked most often

Does a well rule the system out from the start?

Not in itself. The sealed tank and the point where the treated water is disposed of are separate objects, and it is usually the latter that raises the real question. Judging it, however, requires knowing the well's type, its use, its position and the area's water base protection situation.

Why do you not say how many metres are needed from the well?

Because a single figure would not be true in every situation. Judging the distance depends on the well's type and depth, the direction of groundwater flow, the area's sensitivity classification, and whether a designated water base protection area is involved. A general number here would mislead more than it would help.

Does a neighbour's well have to be taken into account?

Yes. For water protection purposes it is not the plot boundary that governs but the actual relationship between the well and the point of disposal. Neighbouring wells are therefore worth marking on the site plan.

There is an old, unused well on the plot. Does it matter?

It can. A well no longer in use but not sealed may remain an open route towards the subsurface water. Its position and condition are worth reporting, because they affect the expert assessment.
