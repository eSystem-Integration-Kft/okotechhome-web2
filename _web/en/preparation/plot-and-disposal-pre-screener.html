Plot and water disposal pre-screener

A pre-screening, not a certificate of suitability. It shows what looks like an obstacle, what is missing and what the next step is — without asking for contact details.

What it is, and what it is not

A pre-screening, not a certificate of suitability

This module evaluates the plot data you enter against rules: it shows whether there is a visible technical or water-protection risk, which data is missing, and which next step is warranted. It asks for no contact details to use it.

What it does not give you: a statement that the project can be permitted, a final product model, a specific price, or a guaranteed soakaway sizing without measurement. None of these can be settled from a form — they need an authority, a qualified designer, or a measured result.

The module distinguishes a genuinely disqualifying condition from data that is simply missing. The two are not the same: the first is a property of the plot, the second of what you know — and only the first entails a restriction.

The test menete

In what order does it proceed?

It starts with the hard gates: if it stops there, working through the detailed questions is pointless.

  • 01

    Hard gates. Mains sewer position, the nature and scale of the project, the municipality's sensitivity classification, any known water base protection circumstance. A non-domestic or larger-capacity project is routed here to a separate expert branch.

  • 02

    Soil and water. Soil type, measured infiltration capacity, seasonal groundwater, wells and the intended disposal of the treated water.

  • 03

    Layout. Plot size, genuinely free area, pipe exit level, ground slope.

  • 04

    Access. Access, vehicle loading, service area.

  • 05

    Data quality. For each answer it is recorded separately whether it is estimated, known from a document or measured. This determines how much weight the result can bear.

The result

What it tells you, and what it does not

What it does tell you

  • Whether a critical obstacle has been identified
  • Which critical data is missing, and how it can be obtained
  • Whether a separate technical element is likely — anchoring, a raised installation, a pumping station, a concrete chamber
  • Whether the direction of water disposal is established, or still to be examined
  • Whether an on-site survey is warranted, and what it would settle
  • Whether hydrogeological or legal review is needed

What it does not tell you

  • Whether the project can be permitted
  • Which specific equipment model will be the right one
  • What it will cost
  • The exact size of the soakaway, without measurement
  • The payback or the running cost
  • Anything within the remit of a qualified designer or an authority

The possible outcomes

Five outcomes, five different next steps

  • 01

    No visible obstacle. On the data known, the usual arrangement is likely. Design can proceed along the normal route.

  • 02

    Suitable with conditions. Feasible, but with a separate technical element. The result names the elements concerned.

  • 03

    Critical data missing. The constraint is not the plot but the information. The result says which figure, and where it can be obtained.

  • 04

    On-site survey warranted. Several uncertainties together, or a question that can only be settled on site.

  • 05

    Expert or regulatory investigation is needed. A water-protection, hydrogeological or legal question in which the supplier of the unit does not decide.

In the meantime

Let us talk about the actual plot

Until the pre-screener is running, we go through the same points in person. Write and tell us the name of the municipality, the size of the household and what you know about the plot — we will ask about the rest.

If you have documents — a site plan, a section, a geotechnical report, a well data sheet — attach them: with those we reach a technical direction far more quickly.

Frequently asked questions

What we are asked most often

Why does the pre-screener not give a clear yes or no?

Because plot suitability involves several factors that can only be settled from measurement, official data or expert investigation. Without those a clear answer would rest on nothing — it would merely sound confident.

Can the result be saved?

Yes, the output is a saveable plot brief: what we know, what is missing, and the recommended next step. It can be passed on to the engineering colleague, to the on-site survey or to the designer.

If the result is „not established”, is that the end?

No. It means the intended solution cannot be supported on the circumstances known — typically because of the water disposal. Another means of disposal, another technology or an expert investigation then follows; the project is not closed.
